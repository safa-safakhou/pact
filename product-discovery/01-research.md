# Research and opportunity map

## The starting idea contains several different businesses

An arena for agent builders, a game with an adaptive companion, a simulation people observe, and a platform for generating games have different customers. They should not share a product roadmap merely because they all involve agents. The first sells competitive experimentation; the second sells an enjoyable relationship expressed through play; the third sells spectacle and collective interpretation; the fourth sells creative tools and distribution.

The proposed opportunity is **consequential, understandable collaboration with an agent that a player has shaped**. The word “understandable” matters as much as “intelligent.” A player needs to recognize what the agent attempted, how their own intervention changed the result, and what they might try next.

This is an interpretation, not a discovered unmet demand. The research establishes relevant capabilities and adjacent demand. It does not establish that any particular new combination will sell.

## What agents actually add

An agent is a system that observes, selects actions toward an objective, and adapts its actions to subsequent observations. It does not have to be a language model, speak, resemble a person, or run remotely. Planning, adaptation, and trainable behavior existed in commercial games long before current language models: *Black & White*, *Creatures*, and *AI Arena* offer different precedents. [Sources 10–12](/Users/safa.safakhou/repos/mine/random_idea/product-discovery/05-sources.md).

The promising change is a potential increase in **breadth of interpretation and adaptation**: a system can connect a player's examples, a contextual instruction, remembered preferences, and an unfamiliar arrangement of familiar objects. That can make teaching, coordinating, bargaining, and authoring situations more expressive. It does not imply unlimited physical actions or reliable general intelligence.

For each design, ask whether a fixed menu of competent bots would deliver essentially the same experience. If so, the agent is probably an expensive interface improvement. Conversely, replacing a language model with another adaptive technique does not invalidate the concept: the relevant requirement is meaningful agent behavior, not allegiance to an implementation.

Four potentially valuable relationships emerge:

| Relationship | Human's interesting work | Agent's interesting contribution | Main danger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teacher and learner | Select examples, diagnose misunderstandings, decide which habits matter | Transfer lessons to unfamiliar situations | Correcting bugs becomes the game |
| Partners with different access | Establish shared understanding, commit, coordinate, improvise | Independently investigate and adapt a joint plan | Agent supplies answers or creates needless friction |
| Designer and adaptive visitor | Construct situations, anticipate interpretations, revise | Find or invent routes the designer did not explicitly script | Becomes an AI benchmark or puzzle editor |
| Co-performers | Make offers, react, set up payoffs, preserve continuity | Respond contextually and sustain a role | Generic banter replaces dramatic stakes |

Ownership can mean “I taught it that,” “we have a history,” “this is my style,” or “I built this situation.” Importing a personal assistant is only one way to create ownership. These alternatives are design hypotheses and need to be compared with players.

The landscape is populated, but public commercial evidence is uneven. Similarity below means overlap in player promise, not identical mechanics.

Additional scans found other agent arenas and game-generation platforms, but they are not used as evidence of adoption. Landing pages are cheap to create. Public signup counters, especially contradictory or dynamically rendered ones, should not be treated as market size.

## What seems to create player value

### Visible authorship

*Gladiabots* makes the construction–execution–revision cycle explicit. *Opus Magnum* adds multiple optimization objectives, user puzzles, and exportable animations. These product features support a design interpretation: the player returns because there is a specific improvement they can imagine making and a result they can show. They do not establish the retention rate of a new agent game. [Gladiabots](https://gladiabots.com/), [Opus Magnum](https://www.zachtronics.com/opus-magnum/).

The new opportunity is not merely faster automation. It may be **expressive intent that survives changing conditions**, with a clear account of what the player contributed. Automatic perfection would remove the learning cycle; random incompetence would make it untrustworthy.

### Reciprocal dependence

In *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes*, one participant sees a device and others consult a manual they cannot match to it directly. *Ultimate Chicken Horse* makes players change the environment they then attempt together. These are examples of roles and actions that create a need for one another. [Steel Crate Games](https://keeptalkinggame.com/), [Ultimate Chicken Horse](https://store.steampowered.com/app/386940/Ultimate_Chicken_Horse/).

A useful agent partner needs a similarly designed relationship. “The human does whatever the model is bad at” is unstable: improving models would erode the human role. Human authorship, embodiment, situational access, timing, and chosen priorities are stronger foundations than deliberately poor agent reasoning.

### Personal interpretation of systemic events

*RimWorld* describes a storyteller that shapes the events surrounding a colony. *Spore* makes creating and sharing a recognizable creature central. These examples suggest that ownership can come from a player's interpretation of consequences and visible creation, without continuous conversation. [RimWorld](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/), [Spore Creature Creator](https://www.spore.com/what/scc).

The agent's memory should therefore earn its place through changed play. A scrapbook is decoration unless a prior lesson, promise, or decision changes what happens next.

### Intelligence tailored to a human experience

Sony's Sophy project reports expert drivers enjoying and learning from an advanced racing opponent. The research on human–AI coordination in Overcooked suggests benefits from adapting to human play rather than optimizing only with artificial teammates. These are useful indications, not evidence that all players want autonomous companions. [Sony/Polyphony](https://www.gran-turismo.com/gb/gran-turismo-sophy/), [Carroll et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05789).

## Failures and counterevidence

The evidence does not support “AI games are all shallow.” On the current Steam pages, AI2U has 87% positive English reviews from 1,021 reviews, while Suck Up! displays 62% positive from 205 reviews and Vaudeville's search snapshot displays 48% from 279. These are different review populations and cannot be cleanly ranked against one another. They demonstrate mixed reception across products, not causation by AI or reliable sales estimates. [AI2U](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2880730/AI2U_With_You_Til_The_End/), [Suck Up!](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/), [Vaudeville](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2240920/Vaudeville/).

GoodAI's withdrawal announcement specifically says it wanted to reconsider engagement and meaning. It supports caution about treating autonomous characters as a finished game. It does not reveal finances, retention, or a single cause. [GoodAI](https://www.goodai.com/ai-people-entering-the-closed-phase/).

Latitude's account of its 2021 moderation episode describes false positives, poor communication, and players penalized for generated content. The useful product lesson is to distinguish the player's actions from the system's output and establish understandable privacy and content boundaries before launch. The account is the company's retrospective, not independent adjudication of every event. [Latitude](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/openai-and-filters).

The *Black & White* postmortem describes an ambitious learning creature and difficult production. A later critic's comparison of the first and second games highlights both uncertainty about what a creature learned and the risk that constant visible correction becomes tiresome. The design problem spans decades: enough visibility for trust, enough autonomy for surprise. [Molyneux](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-lionhead-studios-i-black-white-i-), [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/black-and-white-2-review/1900-6135160/).

Blaseball is a useful counterexample to dismissing spectatorship. Its developer described fans shaping a fictional league and, separately, early popularity without profitability. Reporting on the 2023 closure quotes the studio's sustainability problem. Collective meaning can be valuable while a demanding live operation remains commercially fragile. [The Game Band](https://thegameband.com/), [developer update](https://thegameband.medium.com/an-update-from-the-game-band-7b33e81b711b), [closure reporting](https://www.destructoid.com/the-game-band-is-shutting-down-blaseball/).

## Research environments are not demand studies

Generative Agents demonstrated believable social behavior in a 25-agent sandbox and evaluated components related to that behavior. It did not establish that people would pay for a persistent town or repeatedly enjoy supervising it. Collab-Overcooked identified weaknesses in active collaboration and continued adaptation across tested models. A separate hierarchical-agent study addresses the tension between language reasoning and real-time response. These findings favor bounded, observable actions and latency-tolerant interaction opportunities; they do not guarantee the proposed designs will work. [Park et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442), [Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20073), [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15224).

Outside games, Microsoft's human–AI interaction work supports making capabilities clear, enabling correction, and giving granular feedback. These principles are particularly relevant when a player is supposed to develop trust through experience. They do not mean a game should feel like operating enterprise software. [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction-eighteen-best-practices-for-human-centered-ai-design/).

Voyager's Minecraft work demonstrates a reusable skill library and adaptation to new tasks in a particular research environment. CICERO combines strategic planning with dialogue in Diplomacy, while OpenSpiel and Tencent AI Arena cover broader research and competition settings. These references expand the possible agent roles beyond conversational characters. They do not answer whether a human enjoys delegating the activity: in a negotiation game, for example, an effective envoy could take over precisely the bargaining the person wanted to do. [Voyager](https://voyager.minedojo.org/), [CICERO](https://ai.meta.com/research/cicero/), [OpenSpiel](https://github.com/google-deepmind/open_spiel), [Tencent AI Arena](https://tencentarena.com/aiarena/en/about).

## External-agent options

| Integration model | Player value | Friction and risks | Current recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Built-in adaptive agent | Immediate play, consistent onboarding, supportable behavior | Company pays inference and must handle model changes | Baseline for all finalists |
| Connect a model/provider | Choice of capabilities and who pays | Billing, quality variance, and support complexity; account access is provider-specific | Optional advanced route only if it improves a selected design |
| Import a game-specific agent profile | Carries player-authored lessons, appearance, and relevant history | Imports must be inspectable; behavior may differ on a different model | Strongest portability hypothesis |
| Run a custom external agent | Deep authorship and experimentation | Malicious clients, unequal budgets, outages, setup burden | Attractive for challenge-building; separate from default competition |
| Import a personal assistant's general memory | Familiarity and continuity | Private information, irrelevant context, unpredictable behavior; transferable identity is not guaranteed | Do not request it by default |
| Mandatory external agent | Serves enthusiasts who already enjoy building agents | Excludes ordinary players and hands onboarding to another product | Reject as the default for the proposed consumer game |

No competitor's “works with your subscription” claim establishes permission, future availability, or a transferable personal identity. Any supported provider relationship must be checked on its own terms during technical and commercial planning. The product should survive replacement of a model vendor.

## Opportunity thesis and boundaries

**Lead hypothesis:** players may value the moment when a companion applies something they taught in a situation neither of them has encountered before. That combines authorship, surprise, and a reason for retaining this particular companion.

**Alternative hypothesis:** a capable independent partner makes improvisational, asymmetric adventures available without arranging another human's schedule.

**Third hypothesis:** adaptive agents make it entertaining to build situations for minds to interpret, including designs shared with other humans and their agents.

These are relatively promising positions within the researched set. None is a claim of exclusive territory, a patentable mechanic, or proven demand. The next decision should select the primary player promise. Genre, progression, visual tone, monetization, and Version 1.0 should follow that choice.
