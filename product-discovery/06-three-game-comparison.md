# Comparing the three proposed games

This comparison preserves the three candidates developed at the founder's request. The latest discussion adds [Common Front, a shared-world fourth direction](/Users/safa.safakhou/repos/mine/random_idea/product-discovery/13-shared-world-discovery.md), following the founder's suggestion of process-based multiplayer without graphical character gameplay. No candidate has been selected.

All three finalists now have complete proposed first releases. **No winner has been selected.** Their common technical ingredient does not make them the same product: the enjoyable human activity, content burden, social structure, and cost exposure differ.

## Start with one situation

A fragile instrument must cross a broken bridge. What happens depends on which game you bought.

**In Fieldwise, you are there with your companion.** You previously showed it how you like to reuse available supports while protecting delicate cargo. It recognizes an opportunity to apply that approach, and you operate your own tether while it moves the carrier. A changed load exposes an exception to your lesson. You intervene, complete the crossing, and decide what the companion should retain. The pleasure is teaching, acting together, and recognizing your influence.

**In Relay, you and your partner see different parts of the crossing.** You inspect the bridge; it investigates the power and access controls beyond a service passage. You discover that opening the gate disables a support. It finds a bypass. You exchange the missing information and perform a coordinated maneuver. The pleasure is discovery, communication, and joint recovery. You do not first teach the partner how to be competent.

**In House Rules, you build the possibility of a crossing.** The room gives you two components to place. You arrange a surface and a connector, then let the agent attempt a route. It finds a legal use of your arrangement that you did not expect. You revise the room, improve the solution, or send it to a friend. The pleasure is constructing and interpreting outcomes. You are not controlling a second adventurer.

This shared scenario is illustrative; each game needs content built for its own repeated activity rather than the same bridge puzzle reskinned three times.

## Read one ordinary session from each

### Fieldwise: a lesson meets an exception

You enter a site with one spare brace and two goals: recover an instrument and preserve its specimen. Your companion learned to repurpose objects before spending equipment. It suggests using the specimen case as a support. That fits the lesson but conflicts with what you value today.

You keep the specimen, place the brace yourself, and ask it to use a longer route. It handles the carrier while you couple the tether. The expedition succeeds. You keep the general reuse lesson but add a meaningful exception. Next session you want to see if that distinction holds in a different place.

**This should feel rewarding if:** shaping behavior and recognizing transfer are pleasures in themselves. **It fails if:** you feel you are correcting something a competent product should already know.

### Relay: a plan meets a new constraint

Your partner finds a connector on its side of a station. You find that its lift and your drainage pump compete for the same power. You investigate a manual bypass, share the reading, and agree to hold the valve while it retrieves the connector.

A stalled carriage blocks the intended return. The partner remembers a service trolley it inspected, and you redirect its track. You recover together. Next session you want another place where clear communication and your own actions produce a satisfying joint solution.

**This should feel rewarding if:** you enjoy discovering and coordinating under incomplete information. **It fails if:** you mostly report facts to an answer engine or follow a sequence of instructions.

### House Rules: a design meets another interpretation

A delivery room offers two components to place. You arrange a trolley route, then watch the visitor use the trolley as support for a different path. It completes the main delivery but damages optional cargo.

You inspect the impact, move one support, prioritize preservation, and retry. The route works. You share the replay and later build your own variation. Next session you want a new design problem or another visitor's interpretation of your room.

**This should feel rewarding if:** construction and revision are satisfying even when you are not moving a character. **It fails if:** most sessions are passive viewing or searching for model bugs.

## Economics using comparable assumptions

The following figures use the common mid price reference—$0.30 input and $2.50 output per million tokens—with assumed 2,000 input and 400 billed output tokens per call and 20% overhead. Model suitability and actual call volumes are untested. [Source and full method](/Users/safa.safakhou/repos/mine/random_idea/product-discovery/03-evaluation.md).

| Cost driver | Fieldwise | Relay | House Rules |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Proposed ordinary reasoning use | 24 calls/hour | 60 calls/hour | 12 calls/attempt |
| Inference cost at that use | $0.0461/hour | $0.1152/hour | $0.0230/attempt |
| Example substantial customer use | 40 hours → $1.84 | 40 hours → $4.61 | 500 attempts → $11.52 |
| Provisional all-variable lifetime reserve | $4 per sold unit | $6 per sold unit | $7 per sold unit |
| Assumed average transaction, before refunds/platform | $18 | $21 | $21 |
| Contribution after 8% refunds, 30% platform allowance, reserve | $7.592 | $7.524 | $6.524 |
| Units to cover a common illustrative $1.35m fixed budget | ~178,000 | ~179,000 | ~207,000 |

The reserves require cohort evidence; they are not sums supported by measurements. The budget is a common sensitivity scenario, not an estimate that all games cost the same to build. House Rules may need more operational and editor investment. Fieldwise may require more research into learning interactions. Relay may need more authored encounter work.

These figures explain why none should be called commercially viable yet. They do identify offers that could be viable if the behavior and usage assumptions hold. A cheaper model that fails the experience does not improve the business, and high engagement can be a liability when service costs exceed revenue.

## What changed after designing complete releases

**Fieldwise remains the strongest authorship hypothesis.** It now has a bounded world vocabulary, meaningful direct human tools, reversible teaching, and a finite complete campaign. Its apparent score advantage depends on transfer being enjoyable. This is still a hard capability and UX question, not a solved differentiator.

**Relay has the clearest familiar purchase occasion.** It is easiest to explain as an adventure and has relatively contained content and social scope. Its weakness is precisely that familiarity: a strong conventional companion may deliver much of the same value. It is the best alternative if people like joint problem solving but dislike teaching.

**House Rules has the strongest creator path and the weakest cost tail.** Custom agents have a specific reason to exist here, but the human visiting loop needed construction decisions to avoid passivity. Public curation, a full editor, and heavy testing add substantial business complexity. Its ecosystem upside is not a reason to overlook those costs.

**No combination is recommended for 1.0.** Fieldwise can share small challenges without becoming House Rules. Relay can remember preferences without becoming Fieldwise. House Rules can host an expedition-like room without adding an embodied companion campaign.

## Recommended next founder discussion

First judge the ordinary session, not the pitch or numerical score. Identify the game you would personally want to make and the audience whose pleasure you understand best. Then challenge the favorite with its strongest conventional substitute and its fifth-session experience.

The existing ranking should only guide attention: Fieldwise first on distinctive ownership, Relay first on clarity, House Rules first on creator potential. The current evidence cannot resolve personal creative conviction or show that any of these is fun in practice.

The next substantive step after a selection is a focused design critique and commercial/capability validation plan for that game. No implementation, spending, external integration, publishing, or outreach has occurred. The documents are sufficiently concrete for a game/product team to begin technical planning once a direction is selected; they do not substitute for that planning.
