# Evaluation and commercial constraints

This evaluates the initial concept round. The founder's 9 September shared-world suggestion is developed in [Common Front](/Users/safa.safakhou/repos/mine/random_idea/product-discovery/13-shared-world-discovery.md). Earlier judgments about idle agency and persistent economies must be revisited against its concrete human activity; they are not a founder decision to exclude multiplayer or persistence.

## How the concepts were compared

Numerical scores organize judgment; they do not measure fun or prove demand. Every concept score below is a design hypothesis based on the written experience and relevant precedents. The underlying uncertainty is much larger than the one-point precision suggests. No score comes from a player sample.

Eight dimensions capture the largest differences without counting closely related ideas repeatedly:

| Dimension | Weight | A low score means | A high score means |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| H: immediate human enjoyment | 20 | Mainly waiting, setup, or novelty | A credible repeated enjoyable action and a quick payoff |
| A: human agency and expression | 15 | Human inputs rarely change meaningful outcomes | Outcomes visibly depend on human decisions, skill, or authorship |
| N: adaptive-agent necessity | 15 | A fixed bot or ordinary interface is sufficient | Removing interpretation/adaptation destroys a central loop |
| M: mastery and durable replay | 15 | Little to improve after the first surprise | Deeper decisions and new applications of skill remain |
| L: learnability and legibility | 10 | Difficult to explain, operate, or watch | The promise and action–consequence relationship are readable |
| C: commercial fit | 10 | Unclear buyer, price, positioning, or acquisition route | A specific audience, coherent offer, and plausible channel |
| T: technical and content plausibility | 10 | Requires broad unproven capabilities or enormous content scope | Bounded capabilities and a credible complete production |
| O: operational tractability | 5 | Severe cost, abuse, moderation, or cold-start exposure | Low per-player burden and controllable failure recovery |

Each dimension is scored from 1 to 5, where 3 means a plausible but materially uncertain fit. Weighted total = sum(weight × score) / 5, yielding a scale of 20–100. Originality and differentiation inform N and C; retention informs M; emotional investment informs H/A; creators and social dynamics inform H/C/M. Monetization, scalability, fairness, and inference economics also face the gates below. These dimensions do not disappear merely because they lack separate weights.

## Gates that override the total

1. **Both participants matter.** Removing either the human's decisions or the adaptive behavior substantially changes the core experience. A token approval action or decorative chat is insufficient.
2. **There is a credible enjoyable repeat action.** The pitch can describe a fifth session without relying on novelty or a viral anomaly.
3. **The key capability has a bounded route to reliability.** A wish that general intelligence will arrive is not a production assumption.
4. **The economics can support the intended offer.** Include active users, returning users, retries, heavy usage, free acquisition, and support—not only the median call.
5. **A complete launch can serve an initial player.** No dependence on an already-large world, creator network, or ranked population without a credible cold start.
6. **Outcomes are enforceable and failures are recoverable.** Generated prose does not decide reality; abuse and service failures cannot casually destroy retained progress.

“Conditional finalist” means worth further design and validation, not gate-passed. A concept can be eliminated on paper if its proposed value is structurally weak; measured claims need actual evidence.

The high score for Unfinished Machines is deliberate: it illustrates why a weighted list cannot choose the product. Reliable adaptation to novel bodies is a foundational dependency. It cannot be waved away with local inference or future model improvements.

## What was killed, combined, and revised

Mandatory external-agent setup was removed from all three finalists. The audience should encounter the pleasure before account integration. Personal memory import was rejected as the default; a game's own lessons and history are a more relevant portability unit.

Continuous autonomous worlds, agent-only leagues, and passive expeditions were considered and rejected as the initial direction because the proposed human activity was too thin or the operation too dependent on scale. This does not claim these genres cannot succeed.

Apprentice Expedition absorbed contextual communication from Relay and a narrow challenge-sharing idea from House Rules. It did not absorb their full campaign structure or creator platform. Basic safety training and worker scheduling were removed from the lead design because they create a poor comparison with established automation games.

House Rules shifted from defeating agents to constructing readable, interesting challenges. Counterspell's inspectable interpretation step remains a potentially useful interaction pattern, but the idea is not promoted to a standalone game with a nominal “agent” acting as a compiler.

Curtain Call remains the first reserve if player feedback favors performance and social expression over teaching or exploration. It should not disappear merely because it has a lower numerical score.

## Market and willingness to pay

The plausible first market is a **specific PC-game occasion**, not all AI users or all game players. For Apprentice Expedition that occasion is an evening spent mastering a playful system with a companion. Relay competes for solo adventure and cooperative puzzle time. House Rules competes for construction and puzzle-sharing time. These segments overlap with successful conventional games but should not be summed into a total addressable market.

Store listings establish that players can buy adjacent premium products, not how much they will pay for this one. A developer announcement for AI2U describes moving from $9.99 to $14.99 and removing its token system. That is useful evidence that visible metering is a design consideration in this category; it does not establish the competitor's margin or a universal willingness to pay. [AlterStaff announcement](https://www.patreon.com/withYouTilTheEnd/posts/roadmap-reveal-148966102).

For comparison only, investigate **$19.99–$29.99 list pricing** for a polished complete game, with regional pricing and an ordinary free demonstration. There is no purchase evidence supporting this range yet. Test the intended offer alongside adjacent-game alternatives; asking people whether they “like the idea” is insufficient.

Acquisition hypotheses:

| Candidate | First people to recruit for discovery | Understandable public hook | Plausible channel | Important countermetric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Apprentice Expedition | Puzzle/adventure players with and without AI enthusiasm; players of training/automation systems | Show a lesson, then its surprising use in a new situation | Puzzle and systemic-game creators, playable showcases, targeted communities with permission | Viewers who enjoy the clip but do not want to teach or play |
| Relay | Asymmetric co-op fans and solo adventure players | Show two views and the moment they coordinate a rescue | Co-op/adventure creators and a polished playable demonstration | Players who only want another human and find the partner hollow |
| House Rules | Puzzle makers plus visitors who do not create | Show the intended solution and an agent's valid alternative | Builder creators, challenge exchanges, curated showcases | Lots of watching but few creators or repeat visitors |

No outreach or messages have been sent. Community participation and creator relationships should follow actual relevant interest; assume neither free promotion nor broad appetite for the AI label. Lead marketing with the enjoyable action and disclose the agent role plainly.

## Inference economics

An illustrative comparison uses published Google standard text rates observed on 8 September 2026: Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite at $0.10 input/$0.40 output per million tokens; Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite at $0.30/$2.50; and Gemini 3.5 Flash at $1.50/$9.00. Output pricing includes thinking tokens. The models are price reference points, not recommendations or claims of equivalent capability. [Google API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing).

Assume a total of 2,000 billed input tokens and 400 billed output tokens per decision call, 40 calls in a 20-minute session, and 20% extra calls for retries and overhead. This is a workload assumption, not a benchmark. Large hidden reasoning budgets, long memory, audio, images, tool calls, and repeated multi-agent conversations could all increase it. No cache, batch, promotional, or free-tier discount is assumed.

**Session inference cost = 40 × 1.20 × (2,000 × input rate + 400 × output rate) / 1,000,000.**

| Price reference | Per 20-minute session | Per 10 play hours | Per 40 play hours | Per 120 play hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | $0.0173 | $0.52 | $2.07 | $6.22 |
| Mid | $0.0768 | $2.30 | $9.22 | $27.65 |
| High | $0.3168 | $9.50 | $38.02 | $114.05 |

At 10,000 such sessions per day, inference alone is approximately $173, $768, or $3,168 per day. At the mid reference, reducing decisions from 120 to 30 calls per play hour changes cost from $0.2304 to $0.0576 per hour, including the same overhead. Whether that lower rate can preserve meaningful adaptation is unproven.

A two-agent mission does not necessarily cost twice as much: total call count depends on the design. But if each agent independently makes the full assumed number of calls, it does double inference cost. Voice and speech generation, multiplayer sessions, storage, content delivery, monitoring, customer service, moderation, refunds, and fraud are additional costs. Agent profiles must not accumulate unlimited context by default.

The lesson is a constraint, not a demand to impoverish the game. Use conventional execution for movement and known actions; reserve reasoning for consequential interpretation and changed circumstances. Agent plans can span multiple actions. Local models may eventually help, but hardware support, quality, licenses, and performance must be demonstrated before claiming they solve the economics.

### Sustainable product choices to investigate

| Offer | Advantage | Problem to solve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premium with included core inference | Clear consumer promise; no interruption by payment | Lifetime usage tail must fit realized revenue; requires measured call budget |
| Subscription with included play | Revenue follows recurring service use | Needs strong repeat value and competes with familiar buy-once games |
| Paid interactions or replenishing credits | Explicit cost recovery | Turns experimentation into spending anxiety; poor fit for teaching and retries |
| External provider at player's expense | Enthusiast flexibility | Setup friction, uncertain permissions, unpredictable quality, and fragmented support |
| Local adaptive policy | Low marginal remote inference for suitable workloads | Minimum hardware and adequate behavior are unresolved; cannot be assumed |

Preferred hypothesis for the finalists: premium with included core use. Do not promise unlimited remote reasoning until realistic cohort economics support it. No finalist should launch with an undisclosed quota or a surprise paywall after its advertised campaign. If the measured cost cannot fit, change the product economics or reject the concept before selling it.

Steam's current Content Survey documentation permits several ways of incorporating live-generated service costs and requires disclosure of relevant AI use and guardrails. It says access and payments for such services must be managed appropriately through supported methods. These requirements affect the offer; the documentation does not automatically approve arbitrary external-provider billing. [Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey).

## Company economics without an inflated market forecast

Consider an illustrative $24.99 list-price game whose average transaction revenue after regional pricing, discounts, and tax treatment is **$18 before refunds and platform share**. Assume 8% refunds, a 30% platform allowance, and a $3 lifetime variable-service reserve. All of these are planning assumptions, not verified contractual terms or forecasts.

Contribution before fixed costs = $18 × (1 − 0.08) × (1 − 0.30) − $3 = **$8.592 per sold unit**. Marketing acquisition spend is not yet included. The $3 reserve is a placeholder for all variable service costs; the inference table shows that it can easily be inadequate.

A staffing scenario of six full-time equivalents at $125,000 loaded annual cost for 18 months is $1.125 million. Adding $225,000 for external production, launch work, and contingency yields **$1.35 million** to recover. This is an illustrative financing scenario, not a staffing recommendation or delivery estimate. It gives roughly **157,123 paid units** to cover that budget at the stated contribution before further acquisition costs, financing costs, or overruns.

At 50,000 units, the same unit contribution is about $430,000; at 100,000, $859,000; at 200,000, $1.718 million. These are arithmetic scenarios, not projected sales. Changing the lifetime reserve from $3 to $8 lowers contribution to $3.592 and raises budget recovery to about 375,835 units. If actual service costs exceed net revenue, more sales can worsen the liability.

This is not yet a fundable forecast. A credible plan needs a production estimate for the selected content unit, a measured usage distribution, observed purchase response, channel performance, and a support plan for the tail of customers who continue playing. Finite premium content can be a sound game business; it need not claim venture-scale network effects.

## Defensibility and competitive response

The agent protocol is unlikely to be a durable advantage. Providers and incumbents can add basic agent integration. A particular prompt, model choice, or claim of novelty is also a weak foundation.

Potential defensibility is cumulative: a well-designed vocabulary of interactable situations, a corpus of evaluated teaching and coordination failures, a player-recognized companion identity, reliable replay and explanation UX, trusted content curation, and a community's learned creative practices. These are assets to earn, not assets that already exist. Data should only be reused with appropriate consent; private play should not silently become a training product.

Apprentice Expedition is vulnerable to a major studio adding a teachable companion to an established franchise. Relay faces capable NPC partners from established adventure and action games. House Rules faces tools that make producing and sharing games easier. The response is better player experience and focused content, not racing to support every model.

## Evidence needed before a final commercial commitment

These are proposed design and validation activities. None has been performed, and they do not redefine the public release as an MVP.

1. **Concept comprehension and attraction.** Present equally polished, equally long playable-scenario explanations for the finalists to approximately 18–24 relevant players, including AI-indifferent or skeptical players. Randomize order, record tradeoffs, and ask which existing activity they would replace. This small sample gives directional qualitative evidence, not population estimates.
2. **Agency and necessity comparisons.** Compare adaptive behavior with competent presets and explicit routines on the same situations. For Apprentice Expedition, compare teaching with selecting a preset; for Relay, compare flexible coordination with a well-scripted partner. The question is additional enjoyment and ownership, not raw model performance.
3. **Repeat experience.** Follow a smaller group over several voluntary sessions. Record whether they choose to return, what new behavior they attempt, and whether interesting stories still involve human action. Incentivized completion alone is not retention.
4. **Capability and cost audit.** Evaluate unseen layouts, ambiguous examples, conflicting lessons, interruptions, model changes, and realistic usage tails. Measure latency, billed reasoning, retries, invalid actions, meaningful failure, and recovery. Separate coherent suboptimal choices from broken execution.
5. **Offer testing.** Test an honest intended price and complete-content promise, including online-service expectations. Interest, wishlists, and social engagement are distinct from purchases.

Practical early targets, to be agreed before tests: players can explain the human and agent roles after the opening; most can reach the first mutual payoff without facilitator rescue; they can identify a specific personal contribution; they voluntarily try another materially different situation; routine execution errors are exceptional; and service disruption never silently loses retained progress. Do not turn these into claims that the product has passed. Precise quantitative release gates belong in the selected Version 1.0 specification once the interaction and content units are defined.

## Open issues shared by the finalists

Funding envelope and team skills are unknown. No deadline, mandatory platform, or excluded audience has been supplied. No assumption of abundant capital should be smuggled into a content plan.

Other unresolved issues are: how strongly players want a specific agent, whether memory transfer is valuable, whether nonlanguage controls can express sufficient intent, what failures remain fun, how much inference is actually needed, and whether private sharing adds enough value without a public network. These are the next research and design questions, not reasons to build a large platform spec prematurely.
