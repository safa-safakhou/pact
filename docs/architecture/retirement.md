# Gameplay retirement and identity erasure

PACT-050 is implemented locally. `@pact/kingdom` owns migration `0034_kingdom_retirement.sql` and `KingdomRetirementService`; `@pact/privacy` invokes its product-owned hook inside the same account-deletion transaction. The migration adds `closing` and `retired` kingdom states and makes the account reference nullable on erasure. A trigger rejects raw deletion of an account that still has a ranked kingdom. Run `npm run db:migrate` before starting the updated API or rules worker.

Deletion first withdraws open supply/garrison offers, cancels building/training queues with their normal provenance refunds, defaults active supply contracts under their existing bond rules, and requests exit from active garrisons. It revokes pending contact invitations, pseudonymizes the public handle/name, and marks the kingdom `closing`. The existing privacy transaction then revokes sessions, OAuth/MCP access, model credentials, runs, and approvals. New contact, transfer, supply, scouting, raid, and garrison commitments require ranked participants, so a closing kingdom cannot send assets to a friend or be targeted anew. An already-declared raid or scout is not canceled.

The rules worker calls retirement after raid, scout, and garrison settlement. It waits for accepted engagements, visiting troops, and bonds, then discards remaining ordinary/restricted stock, stops production, removes owned units and installed buildings, and marks the kingdom `retired`. This is a game-time operation: a paused world does not advance it. The account-deletion registry still uses real time; at its 30-day deadline, authored message/request prose is redacted, founding-event handles are pseudonymized, and the account row is erased. The pseudonymous kingdom and accepted engagements survive that erasure, and the same registry is replayed before traffic after a restore. Retrying either retirement or real-time erasure does not create duplicate gameplay effects.

The integration fixture in `tests/integration/retirement.test.ts` covers two closing owners with a declared raid through a long world pause, 30-day erasure, supply default without a gift, a locked visiting unit returning before retirement, and real raid-service lock and resolution after both owners close. Real-time retention prunes retired movement history and, after the 180-day audit window, removes retired kingdom checkpoints with no remaining engagement or contract references. This is verified with synthetic local fixtures; no deployed single-VPS result is claimed.
