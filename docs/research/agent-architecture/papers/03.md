# 03 Integrating Planning and Reacting in a Heterogeneous Asynchronous Architecture for Controlling Real World Mobile Robots

[Gat, AAAI 1992, pages 809–815](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1992/AAAI92-125.pdf). Coverage: all seven PDF pages; main body and concluding claims reviewed.

**Question and mechanism.** ATLANTIS combines a reactive controller, a stateful sequencer, and an asynchronous deliberator. Section 3 makes failure detection, resource exclusion, interruption cleanup, and task-method alternatives explicit. Plans advise the sequencer rather than synchronously controlling motion.

**Evidence.** Section 4 reports an outdoor robot traverse of roughly forty metres, plus over twenty hours and thirty simulated kilometres on multi-goal tasks; the longest simulated run is eight hours. A different planner was substituted without changing existing control code.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** Rich multitask results are simulated; collecting samples and refuelling lack real-time manipulation models. There is no controlled architecture ablation or general performance guarantee. Bottom-up design is explicitly identified as an unsubstantiated conjecture.

**Mission implication — inference.** Cognitive and mechanical mechanisms can differ, but failure detection and execution supervision are essential glue. Unlike Brooks, this architecture retains useful centralized models.

**Test suggested.** Compare synchronous and advisory planning under planner delays, failed activities, and resource conflicts.
