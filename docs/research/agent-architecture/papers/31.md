## 31 — Learning Multi-Level Hierarchies with Hindsight (HAC)

**Source/coverage:** Levy et al., [arXiv v5, September 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00948v5), ICLR 2019. This is the later version of the originally listed HAC preprint. All 16 pages read, including algorithms and implementation; external code/video not evaluated.

**Mechanism:** Nested goal-conditioned policies propose subgoal states. Hindsight action relabeling trains higher levels as though lower levels had achieved the states actually reached. Hindsight goals supply learning signals under sparse reward. Separate subgoal tests penalize commands current skills cannot execute.

**Evidence:** Grid worlds and four simulated robotics tasks; one-, two-, and three-level comparisons, plus selected HIRO comparisons and subgoal-testing ablations. More levels improve the reported tasks.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Without subgoal tests, high levels learn unrealistically distant commands. Penalizing every noisy failure instead produces overly conservative commands. Goal/subgoal thresholds are handcrafted, and hierarchy depth, horizons, exploration, and critic bounds are designed. Results concern state-based goal reaching, not visual general gameplay. Learning curves primarily use episodes; interaction and computation accounting remain necessary for comparison.

**Mission implication — inference:** Optimistic cognitive planning needs grounding in measured mechanical feasibility. Distinguish a skill’s current inability from impossibility and exploratory failure. Test hierarchy depth with matched interaction budgets and compare optimistic planning with explicit feasibility estimates.
