## 64 — Learning Robust Perceptive Locomotion for Quadrupedal Robots in the Wild

**Source/coverage:** Miki et al., [arXiv v1, January 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08117v1). Read main text and supplementary prose/tables across 22-page PDF; videos and figure panels not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Train a privileged-state teacher, then distill a recurrent student using noisy terrain observations and proprioception. A learned gate regulates terrain information. Deployment combines 20 Hz mapping, 50 Hz control and engineered gait/actuator machinery.

**Evidence:** Physical traversal across varied terrain and sensor failures, controlled obstacle comparisons, and recurrent/gating ablations. Covering sensors preserves stair traversal through contact feedback. A 2.2 km hike took 78 minutes, with shoe reattachment and battery changes.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Receives velocity commands; this is locomotion rather than autonomous mission selection. Training uses terrain curricula, simulation randomization and shaped rewards. Unknown narrow cliffs can induce falls because occluded terrain is inferred as continuous. Unusual escape maneuvers remain unsupported. Reported failure-free deployments are bounded observations, not universal guarantees.

**Mission implication — inference:** Degradation can emerge inside one trained controller through memory and selective sensor use; separate policy modules are not mandatory. Preserving mechanics under perceptual failure requires training for that failure and reliable alternative feedback. Strategic reasoning would still need its own demonstrated capability.
