## 36 — Learning Latent Plans from Play

**Source/coverage:** Lynch et al., [arXiv v2, December 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01973v2), CoRL 2019. All 17 pages read, including limitations; external video/code not evaluated; figure panels not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Relabel random windows of teleoperated play with their final observation as the goal. A conditional latent-plan model represents alternative ways to reach it. At inference, a plan proposal conditions a recurrent feedback controller; actions run at 30 Hz and plans are resampled around 1 Hz in this experiment.

**Evidence:** Eighteen simulated manipulation tasks, state and pixel variants, comparisons with behavioral cloning and goal-conditioned cloning, data-size and initial-position perturbation studies. Retrying after failure is illustrated qualitatively. Play’s greater coverage depends on the paper’s chosen discretized interaction-space measure.

**Limits:** Humans supply structured behavior and affordance knowledge through teleoperation. Tests use the same environment; unfamiliar objects/environments are future work. Goals are single desired states, limiting instructions about how to act. Data imbalance and unimodal plan distributions are acknowledged concerns. Results with exact state differ from visual results.

**Mission implication — inference:** Player traces may teach control and recovery without labeling every skill. Evaluate data coverage, alternative valid strategies, goal-image availability, distribution shifts, and repeated failures. Do not infer general cognition from a latent variable called a “plan.”
