# 13 Uncertainty-Based Competition Between Prefrontal and Dorsolateral Striatal Systems for Behavioral Control

[Daw, Niv and Dayan, Nature Neuroscience, 2005](https://www.princeton.edu/~ndaw/dnd05.pdf). Coverage: all eight article pages, including Methods; online Supplementary Methods not reviewed.

**Question and mechanism.** Why maintain multiple controllers, and which should control behavior? Bayesian variants of model-based search and cached model-free values track uncertainty. For each action, the lower-variance controller supplies the value estimate; action selection follows.

**Evidence.** Simulations reproduce qualitative conditioning effects of overtraining, reward proximity and task complexity, including sensitivity to changed outcome value. The underlying animal experiments are prior studies, not new experiments here.

**Limits and alternatives.** Tasks are tiny stylized MDPs. Search error is modeled as computational noise. Neural localization and arbitration substrate remain uncertain; strict controller separation is a simplifying assumption. The discussion also allows partial search, cache-assisted evaluation and softer integration.

**Mission implication — inference.** More deliberation need not mean better decisions. Arbitration should account for prediction reliability and changing conditions, rather than assigning permanent superiority to one controller.

**Test suggested.** Compare fixed priority, uncertainty-based selection and blended control under game changes; evaluate calibration, switching errors and achieved outcomes.
