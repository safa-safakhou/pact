## 69 — Learning Agile Soccer Skills for a Bipedal Robot with Deep Reinforcement Learning

**Source/coverage:** Haarnoja et al., [arXiv v2, April 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13653v2). Read main text pp. 1–19 and supplementary methods/results pp. 29–38; references sampled. Videos/plots/code not audited.

**Mechanism:** Train soccer and get-up policies separately, then distill into one feed-forward policy with self-play. Distillation weakens once performance exceeds thresholds, allowing skills and transitions to improve beyond their teachers.

**Evidence:** Physical miniature robots show agile locomotion, recovery, kicking and context-dependent defense. Training from scratch instead converges to rolling or standing without scoring. Real get-up-and-shoot success is 29/50 versus 35/50 in simulation.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Main results use external motion capture and simplified 1v1 rules. Training needs billions of steps, shaped rewards, supplied get-up poses and substantial tuning. Nominal 40 Hz deadlines are sometimes missed; wear and battery state affect behavior. Turning-speed trials exclude falls: learned behavior fell in three of thirteen, versus none in ten scripted trials. Vision-only results are preliminary.

**Mission implication — inference:** Separate training can bootstrap integrated mechanics and tactics without retaining explicit runtime skill selection. Compare speed together with reliability. Neither pretraining nor fluid behavior guarantees robustness when perception, hardware or operating conditions change.
