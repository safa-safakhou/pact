## 55 — Composable Deep Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation

**Source/coverage:** Haarnoja et al., [arXiv v1, March 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06773v1), all ten pages read, including proof appendices; references partly read. Proofs not independently verified; videos not reviewed.

**Mechanism:** Train maximum-entropy policies, combine their soft action-value functions and extract a policy for the averaged reward. Suboptimality bounds depend on constituent-policy divergence. This composes simultaneous objectives rather than sequencing skills.

**Evidence:** Simulated pushing plus physical reaching and Lego stacking. The combined stacking/avoidance policy succeeds in ten of ten reported trials. Extraction reuses earlier data without additional environment interaction.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Composition still requires policy extraction computation. Bounds assume optimal constituent soft values and shared dynamics; practical approximators need not meet those assumptions. Low divergence matters, and bounds may be loose. State observations include joint/object information; rewards are engineered. The avoidance policy already incorporates moving toward the same target. Small physical evaluations do not establish broad reliability. DDPG also composes effectively in the simple pushing experiment.

**Mission implication — inference:** Compatible objectives may be combined directly in action space, offering an alternative to a single winner or permanent override hierarchy. Test conflicting objectives and approximation errors. Averaging rewards does not, by itself, guarantee that every constituent requirement is satisfied.
