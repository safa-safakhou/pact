## 44 — MuZero

**Source/coverage:** Schrittwieser et al., [arXiv v2, February 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265v2), main pp. 1–7 and appendices pp. 11–21 read. References partly read; ancillary code/data and figure images not audited.

**Mechanism:** Jointly learn representation, latent dynamics, reward, policy and value predictions. Monte Carlo tree search improves action selection and supplies training targets. The latent model need not reconstruct observations; it must support useful planning.

**Evidence:** Separate agents match AlphaZero on three board games and outperform contemporary aggregate Atari benchmarks. Search-time scaling is substantial in Go but much smaller in Atari, where benefits plateau around 100 simulations. A trained policy retains considerable Atari competence without search.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Large training resources and game-specific observation/action encodings remain. Legal moves are supplied at the search root. Dynamics are deterministic; stochastic extensions and imperfect-information games are outside scope. High aggregate Atari scores coexist with failures including Montezuma, Venture and Skiing. Search does not guarantee accuracy outside learned experience. Reanalyze changes several settings together, beyond refreshing targets.

**Mission implication — inference:** Compare cognitive computation during training with computation during play. More search is valuable only when the model and task support it. A useful predictive representation can prioritize decision consequences over visual reconstruction, contrasting with Dreamer’s empirical findings.
