## 24 — Event-Triggered Real-Time Scheduling of Stabilizing Control Tasks

**Source/coverage:** Tabuada, [10-page author draft](https://www.ee.ucla.edu/~tabuada/Papers/EventTriggered.pdf), associated with the 2007 paper. All pages read; proofs inspected, not independently rederived.

**Mechanism:** Trigger a feedback-control update when the difference between the last measured state and current state exceeds a state-dependent threshold. Account for sensing/computation/actuation delay. Derive a positive lower bound between executions and sufficient conditions for scheduling other work around a highest-priority control task.

**Evidence:** Conditional stability and schedulability results plus a two-dimensional linear-system simulation. The author explicitly describes the example as academic. No perceptual game agent, cognitive planner, or learned scheduling policy is evaluated.

**Assumptions/limits:** A suitable stabilizing controller, input-to-state stability with respect to measurement errors, regularity conditions, sufficiently short delay, and accessible current state are required. The simple scheduler assumes other tasks can be preempted. Bounds can be conservative. Monitoring is proposed in hardware; it is not automatically free for a software agent interpreting images.

**Mission implication — inference:** Triggering work from meaningful change can complement periodic loops. Transfer the scheduling principle, not its stability guarantee, unless game-specific assumptions hold. Compare periodic and event-triggered updates with identical controllers, measuring missed threats, trigger cost, latency, and failure under noisy observations.
