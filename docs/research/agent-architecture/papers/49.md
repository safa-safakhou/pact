## 49 — Modular Multitask Reinforcement Learning with Policy Sketches

**Source/coverage:** Andreas, Klein & Levine, [ICML 2017 / arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01796v2), all main-text and task-list pages read; references partly read. Curves and gradient derivations not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Human-provided symbolic task sequences select reusable subpolicies. Each subpolicy learns both primitive actions and a STOP decision. Shared actors learn across tasks, while task-specific critics handle differing rewards. A curriculum grows task length and prioritizes unsolved tasks.

**Evidence:** Crafting, maze navigation and simulated locomotion outperform compared joint, independent and hierarchical baselines. Held-out crafting tasks achieve 77% completion with supplied sketches and 76% after learning high-level selection. Critic/curriculum ablations show that training choices materially contribute.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Task structure and vocabulary are supplied; this is not autonomous decomposition discovery. Generalization holds out two crafting tasks within the same environment. Cliff locomotion receives progress rewards, despite introductory descriptions emphasizing terminal-only rewards. Subpolicies are callable everywhere, without explicit initiation contracts. Missing, incorrect or contradictory sketches are not systematically evaluated.

**Mission implication — inference:** Coarse procedural guidance can bootstrap mechanical skills without specifying their controllers or intermediate rewards. Compare its annotation and curriculum cost with autonomous skill discovery, and separately evaluate planning new sequences, skill termination and execution recovery.
