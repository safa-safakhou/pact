## 34 — Dynamics-Aware Unsupervised Discovery of Skills (DADS)

**Source/coverage:** Sharma et al., [arXiv v2, February 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01657v2), ICLR 2020. Complete main body, pages 1–10, and appendices 15–21 read; references partly inspected. Derivations not independently verified; videos/code not evaluated.

**Mechanism:** Jointly learn diverse skills and skill-conditioned transition models, rewarding predictable, distinguishable changes. At test time, model predictive control plans through the skill space rather than individual motor actions.

**Evidence:** Simulated locomotion and navigation; comparisons with DIAYN, model-based control, and goal-conditioned RL. DADS skills have lower trajectory variance in the reported ant setting; learned skill dynamics support new navigation goals without additional policy training.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Training and test share state/action spaces and dynamics. Navigation comparisons use a supplied position prior. Image-based skill discovery is future work. The practical mutual-information reward uses approximations that are not guaranteed lower bounds, explicitly discussed in the appendix. Planning samples many candidate skill sequences; zero additional training does not mean zero computation or latency. Predictability can discourage behaviors such as flipping.

**Mission implication — inference:** Measure skill predictability and composability alongside diversity. Compare learned planning models with reactive skill selection, including planning cost, unfamiliar terrain, changing dynamics, and necessary but unpredictable maneuvers.
