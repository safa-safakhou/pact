## 30 — Data-Efficient Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HIRO)

**Source/coverage:** Nachum et al., [arXiv v4, October 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296v4), all 17 pages read, including implementation and failed alternatives; derivations not independently verified; videos/code not evaluated.

**Mechanism:** A high-level policy periodically specifies relative observation-space goals. A low-level controller learns to reach them. Both learn off-policy; old high-level goals are relabeled to explain recorded actions under the current low-level policy, reducing inconsistency as skills change.

**Evidence:** Four simulated ant tasks combine locomotion, navigation, and object interaction; ten-seed evaluations and ablations. Goal relabeling helps difficult tasks. Freezing a pretrained controller helps Gather but harms harder navigation tasks requiring later specialization.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Correction is approximate, not a guarantee of stationary behavior. Observations are low-dimensional state variables, not pixels. Goal dimensions and ranges still embody assumptions. FuN comparisons are adaptations omitting its recurrent component; they are not faithful whole-system replications. Navigation uses dense distance reward and success thresholds. Millions of simulated samples are not demonstrated real-robot learning.

**Mission implication — inference:** Training modules independently does not remove their changing dependencies. Compare frozen and adaptive skills, monitor command-to-outcome drift, and account for pretraining. Test raw-state and learned goal representations separately before extrapolating to visual games.
