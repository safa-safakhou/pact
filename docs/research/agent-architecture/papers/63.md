## 63 — Robots that Can Adapt Like Animals

**Source/coverage:** Cully et al., [arXiv v4, May 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501v4), labeled pre-submission manuscript. Read main text, methods and supplementary experiments through PDF p. 25; final references not fully read. Figures/videos not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Before deployment, MAP-Elites builds a repertoire of diverse, effective controllers in simulation. After a performance drop, Bayesian optimization tests repertoire members and updates predicted performance using real measurements. It need not diagnose the exact damage.

**Evidence:** Physical hexapod and eight-joint arm with multiple damage conditions. Hexapod recovery typically took about a minute and eight trials; repertoire-based search outperformed direct parameter search. Ablations identify repertoire construction as the largest contribution.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Fast adaptation follows expensive preparation: 40 million simulated evaluations and roughly two weeks on a multicore computer per hexapod map. Two arm damage conditions expose missing useful behaviors, reducing success to 60% and 80%. Online trials can fail physically. The stopping threshold tracks updated predictions, not a known global optimum. Performance-drop trigger selection remains user-defined.

**Mission implication — inference:** A diverse skill repertoire plus measurement-driven selection can preserve useful mechanics after change. Robustness requires coverage of alternative behaviors; a powerful selector cannot recover a capability absent from its repertoire.
