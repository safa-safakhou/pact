## 41 — World Models

**Source/coverage:** Ha & Schmidhuber, [arXiv v4, May 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122v4), main text and implementation appendix, PDF pp. 1–15 read. References partly read; interactive demonstrations and plotted curves not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Separately train visual compression, recurrent probabilistic dynamics/memory, and a small linear controller optimized by evolution. The controller uses current visual codes and recurrent state. In Doom, controller training occurs inside the learned latent simulator.

**Evidence:** CarRacing reaches 906 ± 21 over 100 trials; removing recurrent features worsens performance. A controller trained in a noisier simulated Doom Take Cover transfers to the original environment, averaging 1,092 survival steps.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** These are two restricted tasks, with separate training, 10,000 random rollouts each and substantial controller optimization. Take Cover only requires lateral movement and survival. Controllers exploit model errors; low-temperature simulation yields near-perfect imagined scores but real performance below random. More noise also eventually hurts. Reconstruction can preserve irrelevant texture while losing task information. Iterative exploration and richer hierarchical reasoning are proposed, not demonstrated.

**Mission implication — inference:** A tiny action model can succeed when perception and memory carry useful information. Evaluate the complete agent, real-environment transfer and training cost; small controller size alone says little about total intelligence or reliability.
