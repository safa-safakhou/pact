# 12 A Review of 40 Years in Cognitive Architecture Research

[Kotseruba and Tsotsos, arXiv v3, January 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08602). Coverage: complete main body, pages 1–51 of 74; reference list partly inspected, supplementary bibliography and interactive data not audited. Claims describe that historical sample.

**Question and method.** Survey 84 implemented architectures and over 900 applications across perception, attention, action selection, memory, learning, reasoning and metacognition. This maps alternatives rather than benchmarking them uniformly.

**Findings.** Hybrid implementations are widespread. Planning, reactive control and dynamic selection often coexist. Important gaps include realistic perception, cumulative learning, memory scale and adequate experimental validation.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** Capability charts aggregate different models and custom extensions; they do not establish one integrated agent or capability strength. Selection criteria and incomplete publications constrain coverage. Most systems cannot accumulate capabilities across tasks without new models, knowledge or parameters. Compilation can also cause excessive rule growth and slow inference.

**Mission implication — inference.** Architectural breadth is a weak proxy for game competence. Human plausibility, believable behavior and effective play are different objectives.

**Test suggested.** Evaluate the same persistent agent across tasks, documenting resets, privileges, custom work, retained learning and actual response times.
