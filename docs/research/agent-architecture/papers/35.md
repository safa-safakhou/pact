## 35 — Accelerating Reinforcement Learning with Learned Skill Priors (SPiRL)

**Source/coverage:** Pertsch, Lee and Lim, [arXiv v1, October 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11944v1), CoRL 2020. Complete main body and appendices read; references partly inspected. Derivations and visual panels not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Learn a latent representation of fixed-length action sequences and a state-conditioned skill prior from offline trajectories. A downstream high-level policy selects skills while being regularized toward that prior, guiding exploration through a large library.

**Evidence:** Simulated maze navigation, block stacking, and kitchen manipulation, including some image observations, three-seed comparisons and ablations. Priors improve over unguided skill exploration; longer skills are not always better. Transfer changes goals, layouts, or task composition within related domains.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Main training datasets come from planners, scripted controllers, or human teleoperation. Adding random data reduces performance. Skills are decoded action sequences, not continuously state-conditioned feedback policies in this version. State-conditioned decoding worsens some results; the authors hypothesize more complex high-level dynamics. Pretraining, data collection, and online task learning are separate costs. A behavior prior is no safety guarantee.

**Mission implication — inference:** Existing gameplay traces may supply skills and context-sensitive exploration preferences. Compare unguided libraries, priors, and primitive policies; test recovery when demonstrations omit a needed behavior and when execution is disturbed mid-skill.
