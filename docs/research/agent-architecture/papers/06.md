# 06 BDI Agents From Theory to Practice

[Rao and Georgeff, ICMAS 1995](https://cdn.aaai.org/ICMAS/1995/ICMAS95-042.pdf). Coverage: all eight PDF pages, including the formal-model discussion and references.

**Question and mechanism.** The paper connects decision-theoretic models, modal BDI logics, and a practical interpreter. Intentions preserve chosen courses of action between reconsiderations. Practical PRS/dMARS implementations restrict beliefs and use prewritten, context-sensitive plans and intention stacks.

**Evidence.** OASIS is described as undergoing parallel evaluation with live Sydney-airport radar data, involving up to seventy or eighty concurrent agents. Other applications illustrate engineering use, not controlled proof of superior architecture.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** The ideal logical architecture is not computable as presented. The practical implementation sacrifices expressiveness; timely option generation still needs engineering. BDI specification does not require three physically separate modules. No single commitment strategy is claimed universally correct.

**Mission implication — inference.** Stable intentions can reduce unnecessary replanning, but reconsideration policy is a central design choice. Formal vocabulary alone does not provide executable game skills or perception.

**Test suggested.** Compare fixed, event-driven, and frequent intention reconsideration under controlled game changes; measure latency, stale commitments, and task outcomes.
