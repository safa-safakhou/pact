## 72 — RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation for Legged Robots

**Source/coverage:** Kumar et al., [RSS 2021 paper](https://ashish-kmr.github.io/rma-legged-robots/rma-locomotion-final.pdf). ArXiv PDF downloads failed; read author-hosted main text pp. 1–9. Separate supplement, figures and code not audited.

**Mechanism:** A 100 Hz motor policy consumes an eight-dimensional context estimate produced asynchronously at 10 Hz from half a second of action/state history. Privileged simulation training learns the context representation; supervised on-policy training teaches its estimator. Deployment adapts through inference, without updating weights.

**Evidence:** Physical quadruped traverses varied surfaces and payloads; simulation comparisons use three seeds and 1,000 episodes each. Adaptation outperforms fixed-context and directly estimated-parameter baselines. Decoupling permits faster control than processing full history every step on limited hardware.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Context changes are assumed slow enough for reuse. Training consumes 1.2 billion base-policy steps plus 80 million adaptation steps, with shaped rewards and curricula. Physical samples are small; severe failures sometimes stop testing after two trials. Stairs and debris still cause failures. Removing adaptation can preserve standing while losing forward progress.

**Mission implication — inference:** Separate update rates are justified when information changes at different speeds and compute deadlines differ. Preserved operation is weaker than preserved task competence. Context inference offers one adaptation mechanism, distinct from strategic reasoning or learning new skills.
