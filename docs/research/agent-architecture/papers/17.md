# 17 A Survey of Behavior Trees in Robotics and AI

[Iovino et al., arXiv v2, May 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05842). Coverage: complete main body, pages 1–16 of 22; reference list partly inspected. Historical library descriptions are not current recommendations.

**Mechanism.** Tasks expose success, failure and running status. Sequence, fallback and parallel compositions coordinate execution. Trees can be handwritten, planned, evolved or partly learned.

**Evidence type.** Review of 166 papers across games and robotics; heterogeneous demonstrations rather than a pooled comparative benchmark.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** FSMs and BTs can express equivalent behavior. Practical benefits concern organization and reuse. Memoryful sequences can lose reactivity; parallel branches need synchronization and resource control; shared blackboard dependencies limit modularity. Learning can produce large trees and reduce designer control. Safety guarantees require additional assumptions and mechanisms.

**Mission implication — inference.** A BT is a candidate for skill execution and recovery, not a complete account of perception, learning or game understanding. Choosing it does not settle how skills are obtained or selected.

**Test suggested.** Compare BT, FSM and plan execution using identical skills, disturbances and budgets; measure correctness, interruption latency, authoring effort and new-game reuse.
