## 80 — Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior

**Source/coverage:** Park et al., [arXiv v2, August 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442v2). Read main text pp. 1–18 and appendix pp. 21–22; references, image panels and code not fully audited.

**Mechanism:** Natural-language observations enter persistent memory. Retrieval combines relevance, importance and recency. Accumulated importance triggers reflection; daily plans are progressively refined, and observations can trigger replanning. Symbolic locations and game pathfinding connect descriptions to movement.

**Evidence:** Twenty-five agents interact over two simulated days, spreading information and coordinating a party. Human evaluators prefer full-system interview responses over ablations. Ablations answer questions using histories generated by the full system; they do not independently replay entire lives.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** The target is believability, rather than competitive play or mechanical accuracy. Object-state changes are partly generated by the model, rather than independently checked game mechanics. Memory retrieval fails, facts acquire embellishments, constraints are misunderstood and behavior drifts toward excessive cooperation. The reported simulation costs thousands of token-credit dollars and takes multiple days.

**Mission implication — inference:** Memory, reflection and plans can support behavioral continuity. Plausible self-reports and social narratives are insufficient evidence of reliable execution, strategic competence or real-time responsiveness in an externally governed game.
