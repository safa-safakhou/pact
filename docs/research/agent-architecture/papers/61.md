## 61 — Control Barrier Function Based Quadratic Programs for Safety Critical Systems

**Source/coverage:** Ames et al., [arXiv v2, December 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06408v2). Read main text pp. 1–15; appendix opening read, remainder not reviewed. Mathematical arguments read, not rederived; simulation plots not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Represent safety as remaining inside a set. Barrier-function inequalities constrain admissible controls; a quadratic program combines these hard constraints with a relaxable performance objective. This can modify actions continuously rather than select between complete controllers.

**Evidence:** Formal invariance/continuity results under stated assumptions; adaptive-cruise and lane-keeping simulations illustrate conflicting objectives and actuator limits. Related hardware demonstrations are cited, not performed here.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** A plausible barrier is insufficient if actuators cannot satisfy its inequality. The paper constructs application-specific force-aware barriers; general construction remains open. Its continuity theorem does not automatically cover added actuator constraints. Cruise control and lane keeping are separate examples; composing their safety constraints is future work. Continuous-time model guarantees do not alone establish sampled, delayed, visually estimated game behavior.

**Mission implication — inference:** Independent controllers require explicit conflict resolution. A small optimization layer can arbitrate safety and performance, provided the joint constraint set is feasible. Merely combining separately reasonable mechanisms does not preserve their individual guarantees.
