# 20 Hierarchical Task and Motion Planning in the Now

[Kaelbling and Lozano-Pérez, ICRA, 2011](https://people.csail.mit.edu/tlp/pdf/2011/hpnICRA11Final.pdf). Coverage: complete eight-page author PDF.

**Mechanism.** Make an abstract plan, refine and execute its first step, then plan from the resulting state. Geometric suggesters restrict continuous parameter choices. Preconditions and side effects connect symbolic task planning to motion planning.

**Evidence.** Simulated washing and household tasks. A representative clean-and-tidy run executes 65 primitive steps while the longest individual plan has seven steps. Results report plan counts and lengths, without a controlled runtime comparison or broad success statistics.

**Limits.** Hierarchy and suggesters require domain engineering. The correctness theorem assumes accurate primitive descriptions, reachable goals and static connectivity: earlier actions can ultimately be undone. The continuous implementation samples limited bindings and is not complete. Stochastic and partially observed extensions are future work.

**Mission implication — inference.** Delayed refinement can reduce wasted planning, but committing early is dangerous when game actions consume irreplaceable resources or close future options.

**Test suggested.** Compare full refinement with execution-driven refinement under both reversible and irreversible consequences; count wasted work, planning cost and unrecoverable failures.
