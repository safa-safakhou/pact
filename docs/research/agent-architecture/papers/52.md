## 52 — PathNet

**Source/coverage:** Fernando et al., [arXiv v1, January 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08734v1), main pp. 1–6 and figure captions pp. 7–16 read. References partly read; image-only results and videos not independently inspected.

**Mechanism:** Evolution selects sparse pathways through a shared modular network; gradient descent trains their weights. After a task, freeze the best pathway and reset remaining weights. Later pathways can reuse protected modules. Reinforcement-learning variants also execute the earlier pathway during forward passes.

**Evidence:** Binary image classification, Atari transfer and three Labyrinth tasks improve over several fixed-path controls. One Labyrinth experiment compares the full hyperparameter sweep rather than selected winners.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Many plots select the best five runs; Atari searches test 243 PathNet configurations versus 45 control configurations. Negative transfer remains, including transfer into laser-tag chasm. Reported “speedup” often means relative area under a learning curve, not measured wall-clock acceleration. Giant shared networks and simultaneous multitask learning are motivations, not demonstrated outcomes. Long sequences under exhausted fixed capacity remain unresolved; task-specific output heads and explicit switches are used.

**Mission implication — inference:** Which parameters to reuse or protect can itself be learned. Test pathway-selection cost, capacity exhaustion, matched search budgets and actual latency. Biological resemblance provides a hypothesis, not validation of a general game-agent architecture.
