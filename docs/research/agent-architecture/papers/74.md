## 74 — Inner Monologue: Embodied Reasoning through Planning with Language Models

**Source/coverage:** Huang et al., [arXiv v1, July 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05608v1). Read main text pp. 1–9 and implementation/experiment appendix pp. 15–19; remaining prompt/example appendix not reviewed. Figures/code not audited.

**Mechanism:** Append observations, actual skill success and task progress to a language-model planner’s context. Existing skills execute its next step. Feedback enables retries, replanning and clarification; language serves as an inter-module interface.

**Evidence:** Simulated rearrangement, physical tabletop tasks and kitchen manipulation. Combined object/success feedback reaches 90% across two small tabletop tests and improves kitchen aggregate success from 30.8% to 60.4% over 120 evaluations.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Simulation uses ground-truth feedback; kitchen object descriptions are human-provided. Real tabletop experiments use scripted motion and only ten runs per task. Additional feedback does not uniformly beat alternatives; difficult stacking remains weak. False positives can conceal failure, false negatives cause retries, and planners sometimes ignore feedback. Qualitative emergent behaviors are inconsistent. No live-game timing or graceful-degradation guarantee is established.

**Mission implication — inference:** Execution outcomes must close the cognitive loop. But adding a feedback module creates another fallible component; its uncertainty and effect on downstream decisions matter. The evidence supports feedback integration, not language as the necessary or best interface for every game timescale.
