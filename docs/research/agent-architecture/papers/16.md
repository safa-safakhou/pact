# 16 Active Inference and Learning

[Friston et al., Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews, 2016](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Active%20inference%20and%20learning.pdf). Coverage: full main-text prose in 18-page article; Table 1 visually inspected. Embedded equations and Appendix A derivations were not fully audited.

**Mechanism.** A generative model supports state inference and policy evaluation using preferred outcomes and expected information gain. Habits learn from the agent’s own behavior. Action, perception and learning share an inference framework.

**Evidence.** A tiny T-maze simulation shows cue seeking, context learning, reversal and habit formation. Eight hidden states and 11 candidate policies are specified; outcome and transition contingencies are supplied.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** Learned habits can persist after reward devaluation and obstruct better behavior. Reported reaction times are normalized simulation processing times. No general game benchmark. The contrast with belief-free control does not rule out belief-state RL, explicitly acknowledged by the paper.

**Mission implication — inference.** Information gathering deserves explicit evaluation; efficient habits may include checking cues rather than merely executing movements. A unified objective is another alternative to separate decision brains.

**Test suggested.** Compare active inference with matched belief-state planning and RL under changing, ambiguous game observations.
