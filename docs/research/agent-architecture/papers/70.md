## 70 — Dota 2 with Large Scale Deep Reinforcement Learning

**Source/coverage:** OpenAI et al., [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680); downloaded PDF dated March 2021. Read main text pp. 1–16 and selected appendices pp. 37–48, 59–63. Remaining appendices, figures and code not audited.

**Mechanism:** Five replicas share one recurrent policy’s weights, with distinct hidden states and hero identifiers. Structured observations/actions, scripted subsystems, shaped rewards and large-scale self-play form the complete agent. Rare teleport/ward actions receive separate output heads to avoid being overwhelmed by tactical learning signals.

**Evidence:** Defeated world champions 2–0; won 99.4% of 7,257 public games under restricted rules. Longer reward horizons improved an already skilled agent. Data staleness and excessive sample reuse materially harmed training.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Seventeen heroes, excluded multi-unit items, privileged structured observation and scripted purchases/ability builds/courier control. Public wins include abandoned games. Numerous choices lack full ablations. Model-preserving “surgery” supports changing interfaces, but a final-environment restart ultimately outperformed the continuously adapted model. Increasing reward horizon does not demonstrate an explicit planner.

**Mission implication — inference:** Shared learned cognition can coexist with specialized outputs and deterministic mechanisms. Evaluate the whole system, including interface engineering and training infrastructure. Retaining prior behavior during upgrades is useful, but can trade off against eventual performance.
