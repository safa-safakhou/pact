## 22 — Using Anytime Algorithms in Intelligent Systems

**Source/coverage:** Zilberstein, *AI Magazine* 17(3), 1996. [Author PDF](http://anytime.cs.umass.edu/shlomo/papers/Zaimag96.pdf), all 11 pages read; cited theorem proofs are external and were not independently checked.

**Mechanism:** An anytime component exposes improving intermediate answers and a performance profile relating computation time and input quality to output quality. A monitor allocates computation and decides when acting is worth more than further thinking. Interruptible algorithms differ from contract algorithms, which may yield nothing before their agreed deadline.

**Evidence:** Tutorial/synthesis with worked examples, empirical algorithm profiles, and conditional composition/monitoring theorems; no new complete-game evaluation. Global composition is generally NP-complete. Efficient local composition needs restricted structure; shared subcomputations break simple independence. Some optimality conditions, such as linear effects of input quality, are acknowledged as unrealistic.

**Limits:** Recognizing answer quality, predicting environmental change, and monitoring consume resources. Myopic stopping is only optimal under specified shape assumptions on improvement and delay cost. More computation need not improve real-world utility.

**Mission implication — inference:** Give expensive reasoning an interruption contract and measure its marginal benefit under actual game latency. Compare fixed budgets, periodic monitoring, and event-driven stopping, including monitor overhead and stale decisions.
