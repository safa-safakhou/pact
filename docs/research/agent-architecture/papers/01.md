# 01 A Robust Layered Control System for a Mobile Robot

[Brooks, MIT AI Memo 864, September 1985](https://people.csail.mit.edu/brooks/papers/AIM-864.pdf). Coverage: all 26 PDF pages, including the implementation appendix; OCR text checked against the architecture figure. This is the technical report, not the later journal version.

**Question and mechanism.** Can useful mobile-robot behavior grow incrementally? Complete sensor-to-action behaviors run as asynchronous finite-state modules. Higher layers temporarily inhibit outputs or replace inputs; lower layers remain active. Communication may drop messages; there is no shared global memory. See sections 2–4.

**Evidence.** Section 5 demonstrates wandering and commanded movement in a noisy simulator, including approximately five-percent motion error. Levels 0–1 and part of level 2 are implemented.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** No physical-robot test is completed in this version. Higher mapping and reasoning abilities are proposed, not demonstrated. A level-2 handover temporarily disables obstacle avoidance for two seconds. There is no universal fault-tolerance or competence-preservation proof.

**Mission implication — inference.** Independently working behaviors are plausible, but their override interactions require testing. This paper supports neither mandatory centralized perception nor our earlier shared-state diagram.

**Test suggested.** Disable or delay higher layers and measure collisions, goal completion, and handover failures separately.
