# 10 An Integrated Theory of the Mind

[Anderson et al., Psychological Review, 2004](http://chil.rice.edu/research/pdf/AndersonEA2004.pdf), ACT-R 5.0. Coverage: all 25 published pages, including task-instruction appendix; numerical equations extracted with font artifacts, so not independently rederived.

**Mechanism.** Asynchronous perceptual, motor, goal and declarative modules expose limited buffers to a serial production system. Learning adjusts retrieval and action selection; production compilation turns repeated instruction interpretation into direct procedures.

**Evidence.** A simplified tactical simulation compares model learning with 16 human participants. Figure 14 disables compilation and location learning separately: both contribute. Other examples model dual-task timing and brain-imaging patterns.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** Vision approximates attention rather than solving raw visual recognition. Instructions use a restricted format, assume known primitive skills, and idealize perfect instruction access. Two parameters are fitted in the tactical task. The authors acknowledge unexplained brain activity and direct cortical connections missing from central-routing assumptions.

**Mission implication — inference.** Mechanical and cognitive bottlenecks can shift with practice. Compilation must preserve waits for new perception and action completion; bypassing them assumes nonexistent observations.

**Test suggested.** Measure compilation benefits and failure after interface changes, with actual perception and execution feedback.
