# 02 Intelligence Without Representation

[Brooks, 1991, author-hosted manuscript](https://people.csail.mit.edu/brooks/papers/representation.pdf). Coverage: all 12 PDF pages, including references; the manuscript reports research status as of mid-1987.

**Question and mechanism.** Are central symbolic representations necessary for intelligent behavior? Sections 4–7 propose complete sensing-to-action activities, sparse fixed connections, local state, and asynchronous suppression/inhibition. Activities initiate themselves rather than waiting to be called as subroutines.

**Evidence.** Section 6 describes four physical robots representing three designs. Section 8 reports at most three layers on hardware and six in simulation. These are engineering demonstrations, not controlled benchmark comparisons.

**Limits and contrary evidence.** Fourteen-layer behavior and integrated learning remain future work. The author explicitly leaves scaling and higher cognition unresolved. Historical evolutionary arguments are motivation, not proof. This paper does not demonstrate general game competence or arbitrary-task transfer.

**Mission implication — inference.** Test complete behaviors early in the actual interaction environment. Do not assume that independently good perception and planning modules will compose, or that a shared world model is mandatory.

**Test suggested.** Compare behavior-specific sensing with shared-state control on identical game tasks, including delayed observations and changed environments.
