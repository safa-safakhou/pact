## 59 — Safe Reinforcement Learning via Shielding

**Source/coverage:** Alshiekh et al., [arXiv v2, September 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08611v2). Read main body, PDF pp. 1–22; references sampled. Proof argument read, not independently verified; plots/code not audited.

**Mechanism:** Compile a temporal safety specification and conservative environment abstraction into a shield. It either supplies permitted actions before selection or corrects proposed actions afterward. The shield retains history needed by the specification.

**Evidence:** Grid navigation, simulated driving, water-tank control and Atari Seaquest. Shielding prevented specified violations and often accelerated learning. Seaquest score stayed similar while oxygen/surfacing requirements were enforced.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Seaquest’s learner sees pixels, but its shield receives privileged simulator state. Guarantees require the real environment to conform to the abstraction and the starting situation to permit a winning strategy. “Minimal interference” is relative to that abstraction, potentially conservative. Convergence arguments require suitable learner access to shield state and stationary replacement behavior; they do not establish arbitrary deep-network convergence. Some shield/reward variants found suboptimal grid paths.

**Mission implication — inference:** Rules and learned play can be separate responsibilities. Keep a shield active if safety depends on it: training under protection does not establish that the resulting policy is independently safe or competent.
