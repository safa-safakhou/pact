## 25 — Feudal Reinforcement Learning

**Source/coverage:** Dayan and Hinton, NIPS 1992 proceedings. [Paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/1992/file/d14220ee66aeec73c49038385428ec4c-Paper.pdf), all eight pages read; plotted curves not independently digitized.

**Mechanism:** Managers choose goals for subordinate learners at coarser spatial and temporal resolutions. Each subordinate learns from its immediate manager’s reward, even when that manager chooses an unhelpful goal. Goal commands condition lower-level decisions; information hiding reduces the decision space.

**Evidence:** A structured 32×32 maze and a moved-goal task compared with flat one-step Q-learning. Hierarchical learning starts slower, then benefits from directed exploration and reusable movement knowledge. Parameters received little optimization, which the authors explicitly caution against overinterpreting.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** The hierarchy and spatial partition are supplied. The partition causes suboptimal movements. Teaching every commanded subtask can waste effort. Hidden context can make higher-level dynamics non-Markovian and even unsolvable; the authors discuss this directly. Learned low-level competence does not guarantee sensible high-level goals.

**Mission implication — inference:** Separate skill learning from current task success, but test whether abstractions hide information needed for competent play. Compare alternative partitions and flat learning with matched experience, including early learning cost, changed goals, impossible commands, and access to higher-level context.
