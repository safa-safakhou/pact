## 42 — Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination

**Source/coverage:** Hafner et al., [ICLR 2020 / arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603v3), main pp. 1–9 and appendices pp. 14–20 read; references partly read. Derivations not independently verified; curves not visually audited.

**Mechanism:** Learn a recurrent latent world model from collected experience. Train actor and value networks on imagined trajectories, backpropagating predicted returns through dynamics. Bootstrapped values account for rewards beyond the short imagination horizon. Acting uses the learned policy rather than online trajectory search.

**Evidence:** Twenty visual control tasks, five seeds and shared hyperparameters: mean score 823 after five million environment steps, compared with reported D4PG 786 after 100 million. Value learning improves longer-horizon behavior over reward-only imagination and PlaNet planning.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** These are separately trained agents, not one policy entering unseen games. Tasks were selected for prior nonzero visual-control performance. Some comparator observations, budgets and reported sources differ. Initial discrete-game experiments are not broadly competitive. Reward prediction alone fails here; reconstruction works better. Action repeat remains consequential. No guarantees cover model errors or distribution changes.

**Mission implication — inference:** Cognitive foresight can be learned into a fast policy during training. Compare this route with deliberation during play, using equal total budgets and tests of adaptation, memory and delayed consequences.
