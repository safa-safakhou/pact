## 57 — The Simplex Architecture for Safe On-Line Control System Upgrades

**Source/coverage:** Seto et al., [1998 conference record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/the-simplex-architecture-for-safe-on-line-control-system-upgrades/). Read the main body of the [21-page author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Danbing-Seto/publication/2312567_The_Simplex_Architecture_for_Safe_On-Line_Control_System_Upgrades/links/54b790000cf24eb34f6eb648/The-Simplex-Architecture-for-Safe-On-Line-Control-System-Upgrades.pdf) through the browser; its exact revision date is unclear. This is longer than the published five-page record. Figures/proofs not independently audited; local PDF download failed.

**Mechanism:** Concurrent experimental, baseline and safety controllers feed one decision module. Deadline misses or predicted constraint violations trigger takeover. The safety controller returns the system to a region where the baseline can resume its objective. Protected processes contain software faults.

**Evidence:** Physical semiconductor-process control demonstrates recovery from bad controller outputs and missed optimization deadlines while software is replaced online.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Safety requires trustworthy sensing, decision/I/O machinery, scheduling and a suitable fallback. Restricted operating regions account for sampling delay and admissible inputs. Deriving these regions is application-specific and remains a research challenge. The safety controller may stabilize the process without achieving its operational goal.

**Mission implication — inference:** Degradation can preserve a defined capability when recovery remains possible and takeover is timely. For games, distinguish staying operational, avoiding particular failures and playing well. A fallback’s guaranteed envelope must be demonstrated, not inferred from having separate modules.
