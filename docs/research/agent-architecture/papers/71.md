## 71 — Hierarchical Macro Strategy Model for MOBA Game AI

**Source/coverage:** Wu et al., [arXiv v1, December 2018 / AAAI 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07887v1). Read complete eight-page paper; figures and implementation not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** Supervised map-region prediction guides a micro-action model. An auxiliary task predicts the next major resource objective as a proxy for game phase. Teammates’ predicted intentions become inputs; training uses their ground-truth future targets.

**Evidence:** Honour of Kings team won about 48% against 250 highly ranked human teams. Forty-game ablations favor the full system over versions without macro guidance, communication or phase prediction.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Uses roughly 300,000 professional/training replays and 250 million training instances, plus extracted state features and rule-based hero drafting. Micro-controller details are outside scope. Training communication labels are replaced by predictions at deployment, introducing distribution mismatch. Human game statistics do not independently isolate strategic from mechanical competence. Its claim that explicit macro modeling is necessary relies partly on an earlier Dota system’s weaknesses; later Dota results do not establish that necessity.

**Mission implication — inference:** Explicit strategic targets can improve an imitation-based game agent and make coordination inspectable. This supports a useful design option, not a universal requirement. Future-objective labels and game-specific phase definitions carry meaningful prior knowledge.
