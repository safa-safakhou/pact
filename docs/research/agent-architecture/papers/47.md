## 47 — Imagination-Augmented Agents

**Source/coverage:** Weber et al., [arXiv v2, February 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06203v2), main pp. 1–9 and supplementary pp. 13–20 read. References partly read; equations/curves not independently audited.

**Mechanism:** A model-free policy receives extra context from imagined trajectories. A learned rollout encoder interprets predictions instead of trusting predicted returns directly. A distilled policy chooses internal rollout actions; the environment model is pretrained and frozen.

**Evidence:** Procedural Sokoban and five MiniPacman reward schemes. Five-step imagination solves about 85% of Sokoban levels; longer rollouts improve performance. Copy-model and capacity controls support the usefulness of predictions. One deliberately inaccurate model remains useful where the compared Monte Carlo method deteriorates.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** Curves usually average the top three agents after tuning; some comparisons use one run. Model pretraining requires additional interactions. MiniPacman policies are separately trained despite sharing dynamics. Sokoban uses reward shaping. Model-call counts do not equal elapsed compute; interaction is slower than model-free baselines. Nested mental retries require a nearly perfect model. Robustness to one error pattern does not guarantee robustness to unfamiliar errors.

**Mission implication — inference:** Predictions can inform an action policy without receiving unquestioned authority. Test whether a learned interpreter actually recognizes new failure modes, and whether its cognitive benefit justifies real-time latency.
