## 26 — Between MDPs and Semi-MDPs: A Framework for Temporal Abstraction in Reinforcement Learning

**Source/coverage:** Sutton, Precup and Singh, 1999. [Published paper](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~barto/courses/cs687/Sutton-Precup-Singh-AIJ99.pdf), complete main body, pages 1–28 of 31; references partly inspected. Proofs read, not independently rederived.

**Mechanism:** An option has an initiation set, closed-loop policy, and termination rule. Options can mix with primitive actions, nest, and carry execution history. Semi-Markov updates account for variable duration and accumulated reward. Intra-option learning reuses compatible experience; value-based interruption can improve an existing option policy under the theorem’s assumptions.

**Evidence:** Conditional convergence/improvement results, four-room stochastic navigation, and simple continuous-control illustrations. Skills or subgoals are supplied; discovering useful subgoals remains unresolved here.

**Limits/contrary evidence:** A restricted option set may exclude the best policy. Options alone can fail a goal between their destinations; retaining primitives helps. Additional options can increase planning cost, and some initialization conditions can slow convergence. Exact-value interruption guarantees do not automatically cover noisy learned estimates. Tabular convergence assumes extensive exploration and other stated conditions.

**Mission implication — inference:** A skill should be a feedback policy with start/stop conditions, rather than a blind action script. Compare primitive-only, skill-only, and mixed control; test interruption errors, new goals, and the cost of maintaining many skills.
